# General Guide: Sports Odds Research Tool

*A plain-English explanation of what this tool does, how to read it, and what it can and can't tell you. No technical background needed.*

---

## What is this?

This is a small research tool for people who bet on sports. It looks at the day's baseball games and points out the ones where **the betting market seems to disagree with how good the teams actually are.**

Think of it as a highlighter. It doesn't tell you what to bet. It shows you which games might be worth a closer look.

---

## The big idea in one example

Imagine two teams are playing tonight:

- **Team A** has won **90** games and lost **66**. They're one of the best teams in the league.
- **Team B** has won **78** and lost **78**. They're an average team.

You'd expect the betting market to favor Team A. But suppose the odds say the opposite, and **Team B is favored to win.**

That's surprising. Maybe the market knows something (an injury, or a star pitcher resting), or maybe it's mispriced. Either way, it's worth a look. The tool flags this game as **"Favorite Undervalued"**: the team that "should" be the favorite is being treated as the underdog.

---

## Key concepts

### 1. How we decide which team is "better"

Baseball doesn't have an official ranking list. We use each team's **win percentage**: the share of their games they've won this season.

> 90 wins, 66 losses → 90 ÷ 156 = **57.7%**
> 78 wins, 78 losses → 78 ÷ 156 = **50.0%**

The team with the higher win percentage is treated as the **higher-ranked** team.

*This is a simple choice, made on purpose to fit the time available. It ignores things like injuries or who's pitching. See "Limitations" below.*

### 2. What betting odds mean

Odds show two things at once: how much you'd win, and how likely the bookmaker thinks each team is to win. In the US, odds usually look like this:

| Odds | Meaning | How to read it |
|---|---|---|
| **−150** | Favorite | You'd bet $150 to win $100 |
| **+130** | Underdog | A $100 bet would win $130 |

A **minus sign means favorite**, and a **plus sign means underdog**.

### 3. Turning odds into a win chance ("implied probability")

Every set of odds can be converted into the win chance the market is implying:

- **−150** → 150 ÷ (150 + 100) = **60%** chance to win
- **+130** → 100 ÷ (130 + 100) = **43%** chance to win

You'll notice these add up to slightly more than 100%. That extra bit is the bookmaker's built-in profit margin (called the "vig"). It doesn't affect our flag, because it raises both teams' numbers roughly equally, so it never changes which team the market favors.

### 4. The flag: "Favorite Undervalued"

A game is flagged when:

> **The team with the better record has a *lower* implied win chance than the team with the worse record.**

| Situation | Flagged? |
|---|---|
| Better team is favored by the odds | No, the market agrees |
| Better team is the underdog in the odds | **Yes ⚠** |
| Both teams have identical records | No, there's no "better" team |
| Both teams have identical odds | No, there's no favorite |
| A record or the odds are missing | **N/A**, not enough information to judge |

## How to use the tool

1. **Open the page.** It loads today's games automatically.
2. **Read the banner at the top.** It reminds you that the odds are demonstration data (see below).
3. **Scan the table.** Each row is one game and shows:
   - Start time
   - Both teams, with their win-loss records and win percentages
   - The odds for each team
   - The implied win chance for each team
   - The flag status
4. **Look for highlighted rows.** These are the flagged games, marked **⚠ Favorite Undervalued**. Hover over the badge to see a one-line explanation.
5. **Pick a different date** with the date picker to see another day's games.

### Status messages you might see

| Message | What it means |
|---|---|
| "Loading games…" | The tool is fetching data. Wait a moment. |
| "No games scheduled for this date." | There's no baseball that day. Try another date. |
| "Couldn't reach the sports data provider." | The data source is temporarily unavailable. Try again shortly. |
| "N/A" in the flag column | That game is missing a record or odds, so we don't guess. |

## What's real and what's pretend

We want to be completely upfront about this.

| Data | Real or mocked? | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Games, teams, times | ✅ **Real** | ESPN's public sports data |
| Team win-loss records | ✅ **Real** | ESPN's public sports data |
| Betting odds | ⚠ **Mocked** | Generated by the tool for demonstration |

**Why are the odds mocked?** The assignment allowed it. Real betting-odds services usually require paid accounts or keys, and the goal here is to show that the *logic* works correctly. The mock odds are loosely based on team strength, with some randomness added, so that some games realistically end up flagged. They are the same every time you load a given date, which makes the demo consistent.

The tool is built so that real odds can be plugged in later **without changing how the flag works.**

---

## Limitations: please read

- **This is not betting advice.** A flag means "this looks unusual," not "bet on this."
- **The odds are fake** in this prototype, so today's flags only demonstrate how the tool works.
- **Win percentage is a rough measure.** It doesn't account for injuries, who's pitching, recent form, or schedule difficulty. In baseball, the starting pitcher alone can swing a game's odds a lot.
- **One league only:** Major League Baseball.
- **The data source is unofficial.** ESPN's public data feed isn't formally supported and could change.

## Glossary

| Term | Plain meaning |
|---|---|
| **Moneyline** | A bet on who wins the game outright |
| **Favorite** | The team the market expects to win (minus-sign odds) |
| **Underdog** | The team the market expects to lose (plus-sign odds) |
| **Implied probability** | The win chance hidden inside a set of odds |
| **Vig / juice** | The bookmaker's profit margin built into the odds |
| **Win percentage** | Wins divided by total games played |
| **Pick'em** | A game where both teams have equal odds |
| **Mock data** | Made-up data used for demonstration |
| **API** | A way for one computer program to request data from another. Here, it's how we get games from ESPN. |
